# Question and marking audit — 20 October 2026

This is an internal content review against the public IELTS formats and descriptors, not approval by IELTS or independent validation by an examiner.

## Coverage

- 286 objective questions: 107 single-answer multiple choice, 88 factual True/False/Not Given, 91 short entries.
- 26 sets: 16 Reading, 9 Listening, 1 language exercise.
- Two original Reading simulations use three passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes and the official total text-length interval of 2,150–2,750 words. At least one passage in each develops and qualifies an argument. Technical terms are explained in context.
- Focused sets are shorter teaching exercises. The language-repair set is not an IELTS test question type. A single main-idea MCQ is not presented as a shared-list matching-headings task.
- Listening uses short synthetic recordings. It does not reproduce four-part, 40-question, once-only, natural multi-speaker IELTS Listening conditions.
- Writing has original Academic Task 1 tables and Task 2 prompts. Speaking has original interview questions and cue cards. They are practice prompts, not examiner-calibrated test forms.

## Changes from the previous bank

1. Added explicit entry rules to all 91 short-answer items. The marker checks the rule before comparing accepted answers, with no partial credit.
2. Standardised time instructions to a word-and-number limit. Removed currency symbols from numeric-only accepted entries and removed an over-limit spelled time variant.
3. Corrected research-meeting item 1: its former gap required a grammatical transformation of the spoken word. It now requests the actual word “afterwards”. The transcript and recording already contain that word.
4. Re-labelled isolated heading-choice questions as main-idea MCQs. They remain four-option questions rather than claiming to reproduce the shared heading-list format.
5. Clarified the meaning of annotation in the first full Reading form.
6. Added versioned marking and content labels. Unfinished older drafts are preserved with a warning to check the reviewed questions and limits; they lose uninterrupted-timing eligibility. Old saved marks remain historical; current questions are not substituted silently into an earlier review.
7. Reviewed under-specified completion gaps. Added 13 shorter literal variants where they are grammatical and preserve the stated fact; tightened the germination gap so the required word is unambiguous. Strictness must not reject a valid answer merely because an optional modifier is missing.
8. Full Reading timers now submit on expiry. Pauses, resets, timer replacement, or continuing without a valid clock remove uninterrupted-timing eligibility.

## Content review checks

The review checked statement/key consistency, whether False is contradicted rather than merely unstated, absence claims in Not Given items, the scope of qualifications, answer uniqueness, completion grammar, literal source wording, explicit limits, and listening sequence. Automated checks enforce unique item IDs, options/key consistency, rule compliance for every accepted entry, explanations, recording/script pairing and full-form dimensions. These checks catch important defects but do not prove examiner-level item quality or comparable difficulty.

## Scoring boundaries

- Original-bank scores are raw correct/total only. No valid IELTS band conversion has been established for these questions.
- Accepting spaces, case or harmless final punctuation is formatting tolerance. Misspellings, extra content, wrong plurals, wrong facts and prohibited entries receive zero.
- Current local Writing/Speaking checks cover a small set of language patterns. A zero-flag answer is not validated as accurate, relevant, well developed or Band 9. No local band is assigned.
- Readiness shows an observed range only after at least two distinct fresh official tests in the last 30 days, under reported exam conditions. Writing/Speaking require teacher or official assessment labels. AI/self estimates, repeats, authored bank items, future results and unknown source types are excluded. The app cannot authenticate user-entered evidence. This range is not a statistical prediction interval or a guarantee.

## What remains unvalidated

Item difficulty, discrimination, distractor performance, reliability, equivalence to live IELTS forms, inter-rater agreement and prediction accuracy have not been measured. Independent examiner review and learner trials would be needed before claiming those properties. Making keys stricter is not a substitute for that validation.
