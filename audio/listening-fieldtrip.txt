Tutor: Let us confirm the plans for the coastal survey. We originally considered Friday, but the transport office can only supply a minibus on Wednesday. We will leave campus at seven fifteen. The journey takes about an hour, so you will reach the survey area before the tide begins to rise. Bring your student card because the driver needs it for the passenger list.

Student: Shall we take measurements on the beach beside the café? It is easier to reach. Tutor: We used that site last year. This time we will work at the northern inlet. There are fewer visitors there, although you will need to walk for twenty minutes from the parking area. Wear boots because the path can be muddy. We will provide gloves and measuring tapes, so do not buy those.

Student: How many samples should each team collect? Tutor: Six, spread across the marked area. Do not collect them all from one convenient spot. Put each sample in a labelled bag and record the water temperature. We have decided not to record salinity on this trip because the sensor is being repaired. You will work in teams of three. One person takes the sample, one records and one checks the label; rotate those roles.

Student: When is the report due? Tutor: The draft is due on Monday, but the final report is due on Thursday. In the final version, the methods section should be no longer than five hundred words. Include a map in the appendix. The report should discuss uncertainty, especially differences between sampling locations. If strong winds make the visit unsafe, we will use the archived dataset. We will not move the trip to the weekend.
